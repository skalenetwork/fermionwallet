Why now

More signers don't make
a multisig quantum-safe

Every Safe owner signs with ECDSA. A large enough quantum computer breaks ECDSA — for all your signers at once. Assets you hold for ten years need a signature that survives that day.

Multisig is governance

It decides who may sign. It can't help when the signature scheme itself falls.

Hardware keys stop theft

They don't stop a forged signature over the same curve — the key never has to leave the device.

Off-chain policy is advice

A policy engine the chain doesn't consult can be bypassed. The last word has to be on-chain.

How it works

Two independent signatures.
One execution path.

FermionWallet is a Safe Guard — Safe's official pre-execution hook. Owners keep signing as they do today. The Guard lets the transaction run only if it also carries a matching post-quantum approval.

Signature 1 — unchanged

Your Safe owners

Same owners, same threshold, same hardware wallets. Protects against rogue operators, lost devices and internal failures.

Signature 2 — added

A post-quantum approval

A designated Quantum Administrator approves each transfer on a Ledger with an XMSS key. Protects against compromised signers, replayed approvals, allowance drains — and future quantum attacks.

Queue

An operator queues a transfer in Safe{Wallet}. Owners sign as usual.

Flag

The FermionWallet Safe App marks it 🟡 quantum authorization required.

Review

The Administrator reviews the payload, decoded from two independent sources.

Approve

The Ledger shows every enforced field. Hold to approve.

Execute

The row turns 🟢. Anyone executes; the Guard checks and consumes the approval.

The Administrator sees exactly what they're approving

Approvals match exact transactions, not intentions

An approval names the Safe, chain, token, recipient, amount and a validity window. If anyone edits the queued transaction afterwards, the approval stops matching and the Guard refuses it.

  • Time-limited: each approval is valid for a window you choose.
  • Single-use: consumed the moment the transaction executes.
  • Checked twice: the payload must match on two independent data sources before signing unlocks.
  • Visible refusals: denials are signed and logged just like approvals.

No blind signing — the device shows every field the Guard enforces

Works inside the tools your team already uses

FermionWallet ships as a Safe App inside Safe{Wallet} and a Ledger app for the Administrator. Risk flags highlight first-time recipients, unusual amounts and transactions the Guard would reject anyway — before anyone signs.

Payroll runs? A whole batch of up to 100 transfers needs one approval.

Security

Hard to get around.
Impossible to brick.

The Guard is the last on-chain veto — and it's designed so a lost key or a bug can never freeze your funds.

Always a way out

Lost the Ledger? Owners alone can remove the Guard after a 14-day public countdown. A dead device never locks the Safe.

Loud governance changes

Removing the Guard or changing owners needs an approval and a 48-hour public countdown every owner is notified about.

Fail-closed

A non-upgradeable Guard with a fast deny-all pause for incidents. No admin backdoor.

No side doors

Safe modules skip guards, so enrollment requires no enabled modules — or a paired module guard on Safe 1.5+.

No allowance drains

approve, permit and transferFrom are rejected outright, and batches are checked leg by leg on-chain.

Keys that can't be swapped

Owners co-sign the key's full public root on their own devices, so a compromised website can't substitute a different key.

Governance changes are slow and loud; cancelling is quick

Even a stolen quantum key can't quietly take over

An attacker with the Administrator's Ledger still can't move funds without your owners' signatures, and can't remove the Guard without a public 48-hour countdown that any owner can cancel.

Leaf reuse is impossible by construction

XMSS is stateful: reusing a one-time leaf would let an attacker forge. The Ledger commits its counter before releasing a signature, and the contract keeps a used-leaf bitmap that reverts on any reuse.

function verifyAndConsume(
    bytes32 messageDigest,
    XMSS.Signature memory sig
) external {
    uint32 leaf = sig.leafIdx;
    if (isLeafUsed(leaf)) revert LeafAlreadyUsed(leaf);

    _markUsed(leaf);                 // burn the one-time leaf

    if (!XMSS.verify(messageDigest, sig, pk))
        revert InvalidSignature();  // whole tx rolls back

    emit LeafConsumed(leaf, messageDigest);
}

FAQ

Questions teams ask first

Do we have to move our assets or change our Safe?

No. You install a Guard on the Safe you already have. Owners, threshold, history and addresses stay exactly the same.

What happens if the Administrator's Ledger is lost?

Your owners can remove the Guard on their own, with no quantum key, after a 14-day public countdown. If the request isn't legitimate, your owners can cancel it at any point during that time with an ordinary Safe transaction. A lost device can never permanently lock your Safe.

Does this slow down our transfers?

Each transfer needs one extra approval from the Administrator on a Ledger. Batches — like a payroll run of up to 100 transfers — need just one approval for the whole batch.

What does it cost in gas?

Verifying the post-quantum signature costs about 1 million gas, paid once when an approval is created (999,247 gas measured). The check when the transaction executes is a lookup.

Which Safes are supported?

Safe v1.3.0 and later. The Safe must have no enabled modules, because modules skip guards — or be on Safe 1.5+ with FermionWallet's module guard installed too.

Why XMSS and not ML-DSA or Falcon?

XMSS is a NIST-approved (SP 800-208) hash-based signature, so the EVM can verify it for about 1M gas. Lattice schemes like ML-DSA and Falcon would cost tens of millions of gas to verify on-chain today.

Can we use it in production today?

Not yet. The post-quantum verifier is implemented and tested; the Guard and apps are in development, and an external audit is required before any mainnet deployment. Request early access to be among the first teams onboarded.
